# Hardware Requirements

## 1. Cluster Composition

1. Each team must construct a cluster containing at least six compute nodes. Heterogeneous setups are allowed.

2. Spare compute nodes are permitted, provided they comply with hardware requirements and are included in the budget.

3. Devices with socketed system RAM are prohibited.

4. Apple M-series and A-series processors are prohibited.

## 2. Laptop-Derived Hardware

1. Traditional laptops are prohibited by default.

2. Laptop-derived boards with processor or SoC packages and memory configurations substantially equivalent to those of existing SBCs may be approved on a case-by-case basis at the organizing committee's discretion. Similarity to an SBC does not guarantee approval.

## 3. Hardware Budget and Pricing

1. The total hardware budget must not exceed USD $6,000, including compute, networking, storage, cooling, and other equipment necessary for cluster operation.

2. Secondhand, sponsored, donated, and previously owned hardware is permitted but must be assigned a reasonable market value.

3. Teams using secondhand market pricing must provide at least three comparable listings supporting the declared price.

4. International teams may use local market prices converted to USD.

## 4. Power Limits

1. The total wall-power consumption of all cluster equipment, including compute nodes, networking, storage, cooling, and peripherals, must remain below 300 W.

2. Each physical processor or SoC package must have a manufacturer-specified TDP or manufacturer-supported configurable TDP (cTDP) of no more than 20 W. This limit applies to individual processor or SoC packages and excludes components external to the package.

3. Processors qualifying through a manufacturer-supported configurable TDP (cTDP) must operate in a manufacturer-supported TDP configuration of no more than 20 W throughout the competition.

4. Sustained processor or SoC package power consumption exceeding 20 W constitutes a power violation. The definition of sustained consumption, accepted measurement procedures, and applicable penalties shall be established by the organizing committee prior to the competition.

5. Wall-power violations and applicable penalties shall follow the SCC26 power enforcement rules, adapted to SBCC power limits.

6. Downclocking, undervolting, and manufacturer-supported configurable TDP (cTDP) adjustments are permitted on eligible hardware. Downclocking, undervolting, and other artificial power restrictions may not be used to establish eligibility for otherwise ineligible processors. Processors or SoCs without publicly documented TDP or cTDP ratings are subject to individual committee approval based on supporting technical documentation.

## 5. Power Monitoring

1. Teams must continuously monitor the combined wall-power consumption of all competition equipment using a smart wall plug or equivalent monitoring device. 

2. A web-accessible monitoring interface, such as Grafana, is encouraged but optional. Teams requiring a separately purchased smart wall plug should contact the organizing committee.

3. All competition equipment must receive power through the monitored power connection. Unauthorized circumvention or disabling of power monitoring is prohibited. Battery-assisted power delivery and uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) are prohibited during the competition.


## 6. Hardware Proposals and Modifications

1. Teams must submit hardware proposals for committee approval, identifying component models, specifications, prices, and processor or SoC TDP values.

2. Proposals must demonstrate compliance with cluster composition, hardware budget, and power requirements.

3. Teams may submit hardware modifications within two weeks following proposal approval. All modifications are subject to organizing committee approval. Exceptions may be granted at the organizing committee's discretion.
